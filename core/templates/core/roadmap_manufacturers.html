Why this matters for manufacturers

Antibody quality is becoming a research integrity issue. Funders are starting to require validation budgets and evidence in grant applications. Publishers are moving towards complete antibody reporting at submission. Researchers are starting to choose products on independent characterisation data. Why This Matters for Manufacturers

The ecosystem is shifting

Our Delphi study of 32 international experts, manufacturer representatives among them, set out actions across the research ecosystem to improve antibody validation. Funders are starting to require validation budgets and plans in grant applications. Publishers are moving towards complete antibody reporting at submission. Institutions are embedding training and policy expectations. As these take hold, researchers will choose antibodies on independent characterisation evidence.

Products with strong independent evidence will be chosen. Products without it will face more scrutiny. The OGA–YCharOS partnership is how you generate that evidence, independently and in the open.

Built on trust, transparency, and shared participation

Antibody performance data affects commercial reputation and what people buy. We know that, which is why manufacturers are partners rather than subjects. You review your data before it is released, and you shape the shared protocols at monthly consortium meetings. That is what keeps partners engaged while the science stays independent.

We do not rate antibodies or name a single "best" product. We identify antibodies that work for specific applications under our standardised protocols, and we say every time that performance depends on the assay and the sample. The data is open, and you can use all of your own testing data in your marketing.

Register RRIDs at Source

Assign Research Resource Identifiers to your products as you make them. An RRID lets researchers identify and cite a specific antibody, lets performance be tracked across the literature, and lets journals screen submissions automatically.

The Delphi panel called this "really, an easy one" and "would be fantastic." It was the only manufacturer recommendation to reach full consensus. Some manufacturers already do it. We want the whole sector to.
How: Register your antibody products with the Antibody Registry, managed by SciCrunch. Registration is free and can be done in bulk.
Contact: Dr Anita Bandrowski, CEO and Co-founder, SciCrunch — info@scicrunch.org

Participate in Independent Characterisation

The YCharOS consortium characterises antibodies independently, using knockout controls, across Western blot, immunoprecipitation, immunofluorescence, and flow cytometry. Four academic laboratories (Leicester, McGill, Cornell, British Columbia) test products side by side, using the community protocols published in Nature Protocols.

Standard model: Annual fee to YCharOS Inc. Your products are tested and you can use all testing data in your own marketing materials.
Try before you commit: send us antibodies to test before you join, to see how it works.
Flexible entry: if the fee is a barrier, talk to us about contributing another way, such as equipment for training.
What you get: independent evidence that your products work. Products that perform well appear in the OGA database, used from 40+ countries, in the training materials of 16 institutional Champions, and in the funder and publisher workflows that read OGA data.
What you learn: which products underperform, and early. The data tells you what to cut from the catalogue, which new applications to claim, and where the gaps in the market are.

Sponsor the Antibody Champions Programme

The NC3Rs & OGA Antibody Champions programme places trained early-career researchers across UK institutions. They promote evidence-based buying decisions and good validation experiments. These are the people who decide which antibody a lab uses, and they are trained to read independent characterisation data.

Standard sponsorship: supports the Champions, their training, and the antibody selection resources the community uses.
What you get: visibility at agreed events, and your logo on the Antibody Champions webpage and training materials.
What it supports: the OGA database, the e-learning, the educational tools, and the Leicester YCharOS team that generates the data.

Clinical Translation Partnerships

The OGA–YCharOS pipeline can identify reagents good enough for diagnostic assays. The Leicester team works with clinical cohort studies and proteomics groups to prove them in patient samples, and to find markers that characterise cellular heterogeneity reproducibly.

Current programmes: Interstitial lung disease biomarkers (MRC iCASE with Abcam), aortic stenosis risk stratification, and asymptomatic heart failure progression in diabetes.
What this offers: a route from a commercial product to a clinical application, with independent characterisation as the evidence behind the assay.
