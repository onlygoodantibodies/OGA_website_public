YCharOS Pipeline

From a gene symbol to a Data Note, one screen at a time

A walkthrough of the whole pipeline, taken from a single gene put through it end to end — twice over, once by typing and once by spreadsheet.

Two ways through, and they meet in the same place

Every panel that lets you type also takes a spreadsheet, and the spreadsheet is always the same shape as the thing on screen. Which one you use is a matter of how many rows you have and whether you are at the bench or at your desk.

In the browser

Grids you tab through, panels that check before they save, fields that save on Enter. Best for a handful of rows and anything you want to eyeball as you go.

By spreadsheet

Download the blank, fill it in, bring it back. Best for a batch, for work written up after the fact, and for the sheet you actually carry to the bench.

1 Orange boxes and numbers on each screenshot match the numbered notes underneath it.

1

Start at the hub

Either way

/pipeline/start/ is the front door. The cards are in the order the work happens, not the order the app is built — and nothing makes you follow them.

  • 1The search box takes a gene symbol, a catalogue number or a cell line name and goes straight to it. Once a gene exists, this is the fastest way back to it.
  • 2The numbered steps are the shape of the job: start a gene, set up what you'll test, run and record, publish.
  • 3Look a gene up, then add it is where a new target begins. Every card is a link — you can jump in anywhere.
2

Ask whether the gene is worth doing

Either way

Type the symbol and press Search. Six panels answer at their own pace — UniProt, DepMap expression, Horizon knockout availability, mass spec, pipeline status, and how many commercial antibodies exist.

  • 1The verdict in one line. TRPA1 came back worth a closer look — read the reasons before committing.
  • 2The reasons, and this is the part that matters. HAP1 barely expresses TRPA1 (0.03 log₂(TPM+1), under the 2.5 threshold) but U2OS does (2.58), and no commercial HAP1 knockout exists. That decided the cell line for the whole run before anything was written.
  • 3Add to Pipeline puts it on your site's list and takes you straight to the gene's page.
3

The gene's page is the map

Either way

Every gene has one page, and the strip across the middle is the whole story: Nominated → KO line → Antibodies → WB → KO confirmed → IP / IF / FC → Reported.

  • 1The strip is worked out from what is recorded. Nobody types a status, so it can't drift.
  • 2Next: tells you the one thing to do now.
  • 3And the line underneath says why — a knockout is what makes a result specific.
4

Add the antibodies by pasting them

In the browser

+ Add antibodies has two modes. Table is a grid you tab through. Paste takes tab-separated rows straight out of Excel, in the order printed under the box. Leave the gene column blank — every row on this panel is already this gene.

  • 1The check counts what it read before anything is saved.
  • 2Each row is played back to you as the app understood it — catalogue, supplier, lot, site. Note Bio-Techne (Novus Biologicals) (you typed "Novus Biologicals"): supplier names are matched against what's on file, so one vendor never ends up under two spellings.
  • 3Create them only lights up once the check has run. That's the pattern on every panel that writes.
5

Add the knockout line and its parent

In the browser

+ Add cell lines opens a spreadsheet-like grid, eighteen columns wide. Tab moves across, Enter moves down a row, and you can paste a block straight from Excel.

  • 1The greyed row is an example, not data. It's ignored on save.
  • 2The least a row needs is a name and, for a knockout, the gene and the parent. The parent is the wild type by name (U2OS) or by C-number. Wild types already on your site are listed under the panel, so you don't create a second HeLa by accident.
  • 3There are more columns off to the right — storage, freezer, box, position, comments. Everything except name and gene can follow later on the board.
6

Check before you write

In the browser

Every panel in the pipeline that writes has this step, and it is worth reading rather than clicking past.

  • 1What it read, and how much of it is new.
  • 2What it worked out for you — here, that parent "U2OS" is your site's line rather than another site's.
  • 3Only now does Create them turn blue. If something is wrong, the check says so in plain English and keeps your typing so you can fix the cell.
7

Or hand the same rows over as a spreadsheet

By spreadsheet

Every panel that lets you type also takes a file. ⬇ Blank template gives you the columns; fill it in and bring it back with ⬆ Upload a sheet. Same preview, same wording, no row limit.

  • 1Choose the file, then Preview changes shows the same row-by-row verdict as the typed route.
  • 2Save these changes answers with what it did.
  • 3And offers to show you the rows without a page reload. The Preview button greys out at the same moment — saved, choose another file to upload again — so it's obvious the file has been used.
8

What is on file now

Either way

Both routes land in the same place. The gene page lists what exists, per site.

  • 1Procedure status counts results per application, and only lights up the ones you have actually run.
  • 2The antibodies panel, with the site chip and the count.
  • 3Apps is what the supplier recommends the antibody for — not what you found. What you found lives in the sessions.
9

One workbook, several sessions

By spreadsheet

⬇ Bench workbook arrives pre-filled with your antibodies, your WT and KO lines and your site's default protocol conditions, with one tab per application. Fill in the tabs you ran; the rest are ignored.

  • 1Upload it back and preview first, as always.
  • 2It counts what it is about to create.
  • 3One line per tab, so you can see which knockout line, which experimenter and how many protocol conditions each session will carry. Uploading only ever adds — the same file twice records the work twice.
10

Or plan a session in the browser

In the browser

+ Record a session sets the header once, then you list the antibodies you are testing, one per row.

  • 1The WT and KO boxes offer the lines already on file, each with its site after the dash. The site is part of the name — two institutes can have a cell line called the same thing.
  • 2One row per antibody. Anything genuinely new here — an unseen catalogue number, a cell line — is created together with the session, in one go, so a bad header creates nothing at all.
  • 3The check reads the whole thing back before it writes: how many antibodies are already on file, how many would be added, and which WT and KO lines it resolved to.
11

Write the results in

In the browser

A session opens as a panel with one card per antibody. Every field is click-to-edit and saves on Enter — there is no Save button and there doesn't need to be.

  • 1The fields change with the application. This is an immunoprecipitation, so it asks for enrichment and the IP assessment; a western blot asks for exposure time, signal and rating instead.
  • 2Method holds the rest — the protocol detail that belongs to this antibody in this session.
  • 3And the same panel hands you the two files for taking this session to the bench.
12

Take a sheet to the bench, bring it back

By spreadsheet

Each session has its own printable bench sheet and its own full workbook, both stamped with that session's number. Write on the sheet at the bench, then upload it to the session it came from — rows are matched to antibodies by Ab# or catalogue number, so the order doesn't matter.

  • 1The session you are recording into is named at the top of the panel.
  • 2The file you have chosen.
  • 3And if the two don't match, it stops you and says so, naming both numbers: this sheet was downloaded for session #502, but you are recording into session #501. Nothing has been read from it.
13

Confirm the knockout

In the browser

A knockout that has never been confirmed makes every result that used it provisional. This is the one step that has to be done in the browser — the cell lines spreadsheet marks both of these columns read-only.

  • 1Click the KO validated cell to flip it, then fill in how was it confirmed? — name the antibody and the session. This sentence is what a reader relies on later.
  • 2The gene's progress strip travels with you onto the boards, so you can see where you are without going back.
  • 3And it keeps telling you the next thing, with the reason attached.
14

The strip fills itself in

Either way

Nothing on this page was typed as a status. Every green tick is there because a record exists behind it.

  • 1Nominated, KO line, Antibodies, WB, KO confirmed, IP and IF — all derived. FC is still grey because no flow cytometry was run, and many targets never need all four.
  • 2KO Validated: Yes — 1 of 3 lines confirmed. It counts rather than rounding up.
  • 3Next: Reported. A gene counts as finished when it has a Zenodo DOI or a published F1000 paper. Generate Report on the same page writes a draft Data Note to your Downloads folder, with named gaps in square brackets wherever nothing was recorded.
