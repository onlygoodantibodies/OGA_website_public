Key message: every figure compares cells that make the protein with cells that do not. What to look for depends on the application.

  • Western blot (WB): a band of the expected size in the wild-type (WT) lane, and none in the knockout (KO) lane. Extra bands that appear in both lanes are the antibody binding something else.
  • Immunoprecipitation (IP): three lanes: starting material (SM), the unbound fraction (UB) and the immunoprecipitate (IP). A strong band in IP and little left in UB means the antibody pulled the protein out.
  • Immunofluorescence (ICC-IF): wild-type and knockout cells imaged together in one field, told apart by the outlines or colours given in the figure's legend. Staining should be in the wild-type cells only.
  • Flow cytometry (FC): the wild-type histogram should sit clearly apart from the knockout one. A secondary-only control shows the background; ideally the knockout and the background match.
  • Immunohistochemistry (IHC): cell pellets: wild-type, knockout and mosaic pellets stacked in one figure. The antibody should stain the wild-type cells and not the knockout; in the mosaic, stained and unstained cells sit side by side.

Tissue staining. For some genes the report also stained tissue with the most selective antibodies, for example mouse tissue from wild-type and knockout animals. Those genes have an extra page at /antibodies/<GENE>/ihc/, reached through See tissue staining under an antibody's IHC figure. It shows each whole figure with its published legend and explains the panels (H&E shows the morphology of the section; secondary only is the background control). The IHC result under each antibody on the gene page is for the cell pellets; the tissue figures are shown with their published legends so you can judge them yourself. An antibody can be selective on pellets and not in every tissue, which Module 4 shows with a real example.
