Key message: if your gene is not there, say so. Requests are counted, and they help decide what is characterised next.

If a search finds nothing, the answer appears right under the box: "We haven't characterised … yet — nominate it". The link opens the Nominate a gene form with your gene already filled in. It asks:

  • The gene symbol or UniProt accession. It is checked against UniProt when you send the form, so you find out straight away if it is not a human protein.
  • Which application you need. An antibody that works on a blot often fails in cells, so this changes what would have to be run.
  • Your email, used only to get back to you about this gene. Your name, institution and reason are optional.
  • Whether you may have a way to fund the work. Characterising a gene properly means knockout cell lines, every commercial antibody and up to five applications of bench work. Funding is what most often turns a nomination into a plan.

A nomination is not a promise. What gets done is limited by funding and capacity, not by interest.

Out of scope: antibodies specific for a modified form of a protein, such as a phosphorylated one. A knockout removes the whole protein, so it cannot show whether an antibody tells the modified form apart from the unmodified one.
