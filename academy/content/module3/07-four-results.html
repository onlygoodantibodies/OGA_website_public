Key message: there are four results, and each belongs to one antibody in one application, not to the antibody as a whole.

ResultWhat it means
SupportiveThe characterisation data supports this application in the conditions tested.
Limited supportTested. The data does not support this application overall, but the antibody was still seen to do what the application is for: it detected the target, enriched it, or gave some selective signal.
Not supportiveTested. The data does not support this application in the conditions tested; nothing on-target was seen.
Not testedNobody has run it. On the gene page the cell says No data available.

The words after the dash say what was actually seen. For example: Supportive — strongly selective, Supportive — detects the target, but is not selective, Limited support — detects the target, or Limited support — some selective signal. They matter. An antibody that detects its target alongside other bands may be usable for some experiments and a poor choice for others.

A result is per application. The antibody in the picture below is Supportive for western blot, Not supportive for immunoprecipitation and gives Limited support in immunofluorescence. A good western blot tells you nothing about how an antibody will do in cells.

OGA's result and the supplier's claim are different things. The supplier's recommended applications are what the manufacturer says the antibody is for. OGA's result is what was independently observed against a knockout. They can disagree in either direction, so read both.

No result is not a bad result. An antibody that is not in the database has simply not been tested. That is not a verdict on its quality.
