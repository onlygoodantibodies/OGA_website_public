Key message: the top of a gene page tells you where the data came from, how to cite it, and what you are and are not being shown.

  • The report. Beside the gene name is a link to the published report (F1000 Report or Zenodo). It has the full methods, every figure and the cell lines used. Read it before relying on a result.
  • Copy Citation copies the report's reference for your paper. Under it, the licence line reminds you the figures are free to reuse under CC BY 4.0 if you cite that report.
  • The summary line says what kind of control was used (knockout-controlled, or knockdown-controlled where the protein was reduced with siRNA instead of removed), how many antibodies were tested, in which applications, and the gene's other names.
  • "Antibody order is random." The list is shuffled on each visit on purpose. The order is never a ranking.
  • Discontinued antibodies are hidden by default, and the page says how many. Tick Show discontinued to see them. Their results still stand, which matters if you have a vial in the freezer or are reading a paper that used one.
  • Filters: Host, Clonality and Recombinant narrow the list by the kind of antibody. Characterisation data supports keeps only the antibodies with a Supportive result in the application you choose.

Under the filters, a short notice repeats the most important caveat. Results come from consensus protocols, and performance depends on the assay, protocol and sample, so in your own experiment it may differ and needs its own controls. For immunofluorescence, it also depends on how the cells were fixed and permeabilised.
