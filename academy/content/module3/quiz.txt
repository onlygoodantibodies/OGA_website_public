1. What does OGA's database show?
- A list of antibodies that are validated for any experiment
* Independent characterisation data, comparing each antibody in wild-type and knockout (or knockdown) cells
- Customer reviews of commercial antibodies
- The applications each manufacturer recommends

2. Which of these can you type into the OGA search box?
- Only an official gene symbol
- Only a catalogue number
* A gene symbol or alias, a catalogue number, or an RRID
- Only an RRID

3. Your gene is not in the database. What should you do?
- Assume no commercial antibody for it works
* Nominate it, saying which application you need and whether you may have funding
- Email every supplier
- Use the closest gene that is in the database

4. The antibodies on a gene page are listed in a different order each time. Why?
* The order is random on purpose, so it is never read as a ranking
- The best antibody is always listed first
- They are sorted by price
- They are sorted by supplier

5. What is the "Example — High-performing Antibody" row?
- The antibody with the best results for that gene
* A drawing of what ideal data looks like in each application, to compare the real figures with
- An antibody OGA recommends buying
- The first antibody that was tested

6. An antibody shows "Limited support — detects the target" for western blot. What does that mean?
- It was not tested by western blot
- The data fully supports it for western blot
* It was tested and the data does not support western blot overall, but it was seen to detect the target
- Nothing on-target was seen

7. An antibody is Supportive for western blot. What does that tell you about immunofluorescence?
- It will also work in immunofluorescence
* Nothing. Each result belongs to one application, so check the immunofluorescence result separately
- It will not work in immunofluorescence
- Immunofluorescence was not tested

8. What does the IHC result on an OGA gene page rest on?
- The supplier's own IHC images
- A single wild-type tissue section with no control
* Wild-type, knockout and mosaic cell pellets, stained side by side
- Customer reviews

9. An antibody you want to use is not in the OGA database. What does that tell you about it?
- It performed badly and was removed
- It is discontinued
* Only that it has not been characterised, which is not a verdict on its quality
- It is not specific

10. How should you cite an antibody in your methods?
- By its catalogue number alone
- By the supplier's name alone
* With its RRID, e.g. "Proteintech Cat# 10269-1-AP, RRID:AB_2193750"
- By the gene name
